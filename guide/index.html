For developers

Dungeon Crawler Field Guide

How the game is put together: where the code lives, how its worlds are generated, how enemies find you, how a hit is decided, how a crayon drawing becomes a monster, and how the online arena stays in step. The stages on this page run the game's own algorithms and constants, ported from the source.

  • Electron + vanilla JS, no bundler
  • 32 px tiles
  • systems are DOM-free
  • tests on node:test
  • PvP simulation at 30 Hz

Code layout

The Electron shell (main.cjs, preload.cjs) hosts one page, renderer/index.html, and renderer/game.js runs it. game.js owns input, the frame loop and the seams between modes and saves. Everything it calls is split by job, and the split is strict: a system holds rules and never touches the DOM, a render module draws and never decides, and tuning numbers sit in data tables.

PathWhat lives there
renderer/game.jsThe orchestrator: keys and touch, update and render, building entities from spawn lists, switching mode and save.
renderer/systems/Gameplay rules, one module per system: map generation, navigation, brain, stamina, shield, spells, loot, lumber, quests. Pure, so each has a unit test.
renderer/render/Canvas drawing: tile art, the chunk cache, sprites, monster rigs, weather, HUD, synthesised audio.
renderer/data/Tables: level configs, enemy AI rows, monsters (one JSON each), tales, PvP and netcode numbers.
renderer/wilds/, renderer/terrain/The endless outdoor world and the tile-laying library it shares with the hand-made maps.
renderer/pvp/, renderer/net/, server/The arena simulation, client netcode, and the WebSocket server that runs the same simulation in Node.
tools/Editors, generators, extractors, benches and probes. Never shipped; game code may not import from here.
test/About 220 node:test files, one per system, with playwright-core for canvas checks.

One frame

gameLoop clamps the frame time to 100 ms, so a hitch never lets anything tunnel through a wall, and only the playing phase animates. Sound cues are drained every frame, paused or not.

  1. Re-centre the WildstickWilds runs before anything reads the map, then the tile cache is re-keyed.
  2. PlayerBinds, shield, movement, stamina, then the swing, shot or cast.
  3. Projectiles and groundstepProjectiles, bramble and fire zones, lightning marks.
  4. Everyone elseVillagers, then for each enemy act(e, updateBrain(e)), then enemy attacks.
  5. SightmaybeComputeFOV: radius 8 with line of sight underground, 14 and open on the surface.
  6. DrawTile chunks, entities, monster effects, health bars, night wash, HUD.

Dungeon generator

Dungeon Rush levels and the caves under the Wilds come from generateLevel. It splits the map into leaves, puts a room in each, joins the rooms with a minimum spanning tree, stamps the boss lair over a room, and then repairs whatever the lair cut off. Step through it.

The repair step matters more than it looks. A lair is mostly wall and lands on corridors that were already carved, so it can leave fifteen separate regions. healConnectivity gets a budget of regions minus one, because each pass joins at least two. A level that is still in pieces is thrown away, and after five failures the game falls back to one empty room without a warning. If content goes missing at random, look for a connectivity bug first.

The Wilds

Adventure is an endless outdoor world built from 80 × 80 tile pieces. Nothing in it is generated from a random stream. Every answer is a pure function of the seed and a coordinate, so any piece can be made at any time, in any order, and still fit its neighbours.

The big picture never moves

layout.js decides the country at every tile: forest around home, then one country per compass direction with the borders bent by noise, and a blob of other country in every 3 × 3 pieces. Toughness rises with distance from home, and each 4 × 4 block of pieces holds exactly one village. Change the seed and move over the map.

What a piece holds

A piece has a deal, a counter that says which version of its interior to generate. The outer six tiles never depend on the deal, and the next eight blend from the fixed frame to the dealt interior. That is what lets a forgotten piece come back different and still join up.

The game still runs on an ordinary map

No system knows the world is endless. The game sees a 240 × 240 map: a 3 × 3 window of pieces whose rows share the pieces' own cell objects. When the player is eight tiles into a neighbouring piece the window steps over (24 in Kalevanlinna, so the square, where its four pieces meet, holds still). shiftState then moves every tile and pixel position it can find in the state by 80 tiles, and the baked tile chunks are re-keyed instead of redrawn. Click or tap to walk.

Tile cache

Electron here falls back to a software canvas, so the frame budget is the number of draw calls. Drawing every visible cell costs about 700 sprite blits a frame. The tile layer bakes the map into 16 × 16-cell images instead and blits one image per visible chunk. A chunk is baked again only when its count of explored cells changes or a system marks one of its cells dirty.

The Wilds window holds 225 chunks at a megabyte each, so the cache keeps at most 64 and drops the farthest from the view. During a mushroom trip each chunk keeps a hue-rotated copy, and only two copies are rebuilt per frame.

Enemy AI

Three modules, three questions. nav.js answers where can a body go. brain.js answers what does it want and returns an intent. act.js answers how does it move: it follows a path or a field, pushes enemies apart, and slides along walls one axis at a time.

One field for every chaser

Chasers do not each plan a path. One Dijkstra field holds the walking distance from every tile to the player, and it is rebuilt only when the player's tile changes. A chaser steps to the neighbour with the lowest number, and a fleeing enemy steps to the highest. Body width is handled by a clearance grid: the distance from each tile to the nearest wall. A two-tile body may use only tiles with clearance 2, so narrow doors close for it.

A* is still there for fixed targets: patrol points and the last place the player was seen. It reuses its scratch arrays, repaths at most every half second, and skips a waypoint when the body can slide straight to the next one. In the Wilds the field stops 48 tiles out and anything more than 34 tiles from the player sleeps.

What an enemy wants

Who routs is data. In data/enemy-ai.js a humanoid or mammal flees at 30 % health and a beast fights to the death, unless the row says otherwise. A monster from the registry brings its own row in its JSON, which replaces the built-in row whole.

Hits and blocks

Every body resolves to one shape: a capsule, which is a segment with a radius. A circle is a capsule of zero length. Each damage path asks the shape one of three questions, and movement asks none of them, because how wide a body is for walking is a navigation matter.

One stamina tank prices everything: 100 points, refilling at 18 a second after 0.7 s without spending. A dagger swing costs 8 and an overcharged axe 48. A release the tank cannot afford drops to the dearest tier it can, so a long hold is never wasted. A block costs the shield's own price, and a block that empties the tank drops the shield for 0.8 s.

Lives in renderer/systems/hitbox.js capsules.js shield.js stamina.js player-damage.js

Chest loot

Two things shape a chest. The level's loot tier picks the pool, on one ladder laid over every depth scale. What the player wears decides whether a category is worth offering: bows need the Ranger's Coat, wands the Mage's Robe, heavy blades the Plated Armor. A category the player cannot use keeps a fifth of its weight and offers the humblest item of its kind, enough to hint that it exists.

Monster pipeline

A monster is a JSON file plus a rig, and nobody has to write the rig. A drawing is cut into pieces, the pieces are packed into an atlas, and one shared engine swings them. Re-cutting rewrites data only. Monsteri, the depth-4 boss, started as a child's crayon drawing.

  1. DrawingA PNG or a photo of paper. The mask keys out the backdrop by flooding in from the border.
  2. CutThe finder takes the thick core as the torso and cuts each limb where it leaves the core. You correct it by hand. assets/cuts/<name>.json
  3. BuildPieces, pivots and joint caps packed into <name>-parts.png and .js.
  4. Rigcutout.js turns each piece about its pivot, parent before child.
  5. BenchAn arena with the monster and live sliders for look, stats and its hook's tuning.
  6. PublishMonster Studio keeps drafts apart from the game until you publish.

The cut-out engine

Motion is stepped, four poses to a gait cycle, which suits pixel art. Each role has a rule. In a front view both arms turn the same way, so one swings out as the other comes in, and the feet lift in turn. The sliders below are the rig's real parameters with their real ranges.

Behaviour is separate from looks. A hook module in systems/monsters/ runs the monster's state machine and may export a TUNING_SCHEMA; the bench reads it and turns every number into a slider that applies on the next frame. For posed animation, Studio's Movement tab stores pose cards in the def, and the attack's hit card sets the weapon's real windup.

Village tales

Every village has a tale that fits the land within three pieces of it. The tale's place has no spot on the map at first. When new land enters the window, admit may let one of those pieces become the place. How exact the villagers are depends on the tale's kind.

KindWhat villagers tell youHow the place appearsTales
needExactly whereTakes the nearest fitting piece once its village is nearTar pit, ferryman's bell, the fold, Lalli
storyA compass sideRolls with rising odds; certain by the second piece on its sideThe Elk of Hiisi
oldOnly the kind of landRolls with rising odds; certain by the fifth pieceKivihiisi, the flame-thief

A place is stamped as the main thing of its piece, with named points that match the labels the quest code already used on the hand-made maps. That is how tale-run.js runs the old Adventure quest modules unchanged: one context per tale, flags kept in the world record, points converted to window tiles at each use, and its creatures rebuilt from flags when their piece returns. Two places of one kind stay at least three pieces apart, because quest code finds its creature by type and a window must never hold two.

Online arena

The arena is a fixed-step simulation of two to six heroes, renderer/pvp/sim.js, with no DOM in it. The server imports that same file and steps it 30 times a second, and sends snapshots 20 times a second. The client hides the delay in two different ways.

Your own hero is predicted. Each input is applied at once with the same moveHero the server runs, and kept in a pending list. When a snapshot arrives the hero is reset to the server's state and the inputs the server has not acknowledged are replayed. If the result differs from what was on screen by 2 to 96 px, the difference is blended away over 100 ms. Everyone else is interpolated three ticks behind the estimated server tick, between the two snapshots around that moment.

Damage is never predicted, but the stamina and cooldown a swing pays are, so a replay leaves the tank where the server has it. Melee is lag-compensated on the server: a swing is tested against where the attacker saw the target, up to nine ticks (300 ms) back. The interpolation clock is slewed at most 10 % toward each new estimate and never runs backwards.

House rules

Each of these was learned from a bug.

  • Wilds cells are pure in their world tile. Never use a sequential random stream for anything a neighbouring piece can see. test/wilds-terrain.test.js holds the frame to it.
  • Bump GEN in wilds/world.js whenever the generator's output changes. Old saves then drop their records and wake at the last village.
  • Call markTileDirty after changing a cell's tile, overlay or skin, or the baked chunk keeps showing the old art.
  • Convert world tiles to window tiles at the moment of use. A window tile held across frames is wrong after the next re-centre.
  • Heal connectivity last. Any step that stamps floor or wall after the heal can strand a region, and five failed levels become one empty room.
  • An unknown spawn kind is dropped silently by buildEntities. Add new kinds to its switch and to the arena's allow-list.
  • Every weapon key needs a SPRITES['weapon_<key>'] entry or the canvas draws nothing. test/sprites.test.js guards it.
  • No built-in AI row may share a name with a registry monster. Registration replaces the row whole.
  • Keep the arena simulation DOM-free. The server runs the same files in Node, and the predictor must spend exactly what the server spends.
  • Count draw calls. On a software canvas a new per-cell effect is a frame-rate bug until measured.
  • Leave TRUST_PROXY=1 in the Dockerfile. Without it every player shares one address and the per-IP limits lock out online play.
